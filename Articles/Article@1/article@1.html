James Webb Space Telescope first image: A Glimpse into the Ancient Universe.

By Shankar Dahal | May 13, 2025

Ever since the launch of the Hubble space telescope, scientists are peering deeper and deeper into the past of our universe. Among the countless regions in the universe, scientists focused on a small patch in constellation Volans called SMACS 0723, which contains a galaxy cluster about 4 billion light-years from Earth. The limitation of the Hubble space telescope, which operates on the visible spectrum, restricted the information it could provide, as light passes through gas and dust and thus giving us a limited understanding.

The James Webb Space Telescope was launched in December 2021 and began operating in July of 2022, was hailed as a “successor” of The Hubble Space Telescope. It was unique because, unlike the Hubble Space Telescope, James Webb operates in the Near-Infrared spectrum which won’t be blocked by gas and dust allowing it to see through cosmic dust and reveal previously hidden regions of the universe.

As Webb peered into space, it revealed information that astonished many astronomers. Thousands of galaxies are visible in the image and among those many are as old as the universe itself. Webb’s sharp near-infrared vision captured the faint structures in extremely distant galaxies in unprecedented detail. Some of these galaxies date back as fas as 13 billion years. It is the highest resolution image of the early universe ever taken.

The image showed a galaxy cluster SMACS 0723 as it appeared 4.6 billion years ago, covering an arc of sky with an angular size of 2.4 arcminutes. Many of these galaxies in the image have undergone notable redshift due to the expansion of space. The combined mass of the galaxy cluster acts as a gravitational lens, magnifying and distorting the images of much more distant galaxies behind it.

The image was unveiled to the public on 11 July 2022 by U.S. President Joe Biden during a special event.

The difference in resolution and detail is astounding, with Webb revealing thousands of galaxies that were previously hidden from view.
